## 1. Homepage hero

> **Florist in Enfield, North London**
>
> # Flowers for the people you love, made by hand and brought to their door
>
> I make up fresh bouquets and balloon gifts here in Enfield and deliver them by hand the same day across North
> London. If you order by 10am, they can be with your someone special this afternoon, with your card tucked
> inside.
>
> *[Choose today's flowers]*   *[Add a balloon gift]*
>
> It all began with jasmine crowns in Cyprus [her words: one line about that memory]. I still feel that same
> little thrill every time I wrap a bouquet for someone. [to confirm: her feeling, in her words]

**Why it's written this way:** the first two sentences answer what she does, where and by when, so Google and
assistants can quote them, but they read as her talking, not as a delivery service. "Your someone special" keeps
the person receiving the flowers at the centre. The cut-off is a kind reminder, not a countdown. The founder line
is a placeholder until she gives her own words.

## 3. A crystal guide opener (rose quartz)

> # What does rose quartz mean?
>
> Rose quartz is the soft pink stone that has long been given as a token of love, and in crystal folklore it's
> known as the stone of the heart. It makes a gentle gift for a partner, a mum or a dear friend, and it sits
> beautifully beside a bouquet.
>
> Here I'll share what it looks like, where it comes from, the traditions that have grown up around it, and how
> to look after it so its colour stays soft and pretty for years.

**Why it's written this way:** the first sentence answers the question, through tradition ("long been given",
"in crystal folklore"), never as an effect. That keeps it within the ASA rules: no healing, calming or attracting
love. It ends on care, which is practical and true (rose quartz can fade in strong sunlight). The page closes
with the standard crystal disclaimer.
