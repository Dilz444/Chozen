# ChoZen Boutique: UK legal and advertising compliance (October 2026)

> **Not legal advice.** This summary is for an online florist and gift shop in Enfield, England. Where it says **[Solicitor]** or **[TS]**, check the point with a solicitor or with Enfield Trading Standards, which offers paid Primary Authority advice through Business Companion. **Network note:** gov.uk, legislation.gov.uk, asa.org.uk and ico.org.uk were blocked from this environment, so I could not read them directly. Points marked **(secondary)** come from law-firm or trade summaries found by search. Check them against the official page before relying on them.

## 1. Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 (CCRs) and Consumer Rights Act 2015 (CRA)

**Information required before purchase (CCRs Sch. 2).** Before the customer orders, tell them clearly:
- the main characteristics of each item
- your identity, geographic address, email and phone
- the total price including VAT and delivery
- payment and delivery arrangements, including the delivery date or window
- whether there is a right to cancel and how to use it, or why it does not apply (for example, fresh flowers)
- who pays return costs
- a reminder that the customer has legal rights if goods are faulty
- for subscriptions, the duration and how to end it

The final checkout button must make clear that the customer is agreeing to pay, for example **"Order with obligation to pay"** or **"Pay now"** (reg 14). Send an order confirmation that contains this information in a durable form, such as email. BIS guidance: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a817b92ed915d74e33fe73a/bis-13-1368-consumer-contracts-information-cancellation-and-additional-payments-regulations-guidance.pdf

**14-day cancellation right (online goods).** The customer can cancel within 14 days of receiving the goods, without giving a reason. If you do not tell them about this right, it is extended by up to 12 months.

**Exemptions (reg 28)** (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/part/3, https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/exemptions-right-cancel-consumer-contracts):
- **Fresh flowers and balloon bouquets with fresh flowers:** "goods liable to deteriorate or expire rapidly". Official guidance gives flowers as an example. Say this on the product page and in your Returns policy. Helium balloons on their own are not clearly covered by this exemption **[Solicitor]**.
- **Made to the customer's specification or clearly personalised:** for example engraved jewellery or a bespoke faux arrangement. Standard catalogue items are *not* exempt just because you make them to order.
- **Sealed goods unsealed after delivery, where the seal is there for health or hygiene reasons:** this may cover earrings for pierced ears if you supply them sealed and say so beforehand **(secondary/[TS])**.
- Faux florals, crystals, other jewellery and general gifts: the full 14-day right applies.

**Refunds.** Refund within 14 days of getting the goods back, or of receiving evidence that they were sent back, whichever comes first. Refund the original standard delivery charge as well. You may deduct an amount if the customer handled the goods more than was needed to check them.

**Return costs.** The customer pays to send goods back only if you told them so before they bought. Otherwise you pay.

**Model cancellation form.** Include the model form from CCRs Sch. 3 (or a link to it) in your terms or order confirmation.

**Subscriptions under the CCRs today.** For goods delivered regularly, the 14-day period runs from delivery of the first box. Fresh-flower deliveries are exempt as perishable. Even so, let customers cancel future deliveries easily. Whether the perishable exemption covers the whole subscription contract is not settled **[Solicitor]**. See the DMCC subscription rules below, which take over from January 2027.

**Floral hire (a service).** Consumers have 14 days from the contract date to cancel. If they ask you to start within that period, they owe a proportionate amount for what you have already provided. Reg 28(1)(b) excludes leisure-related services booked for a specific date, which may cover hire for a particular event **[Solicitor]**. Hire to businesses (offices, restaurants) is B2B, so the CCRs do not apply and your contract terms govern instead.

**Consumer Rights Act 2015.** Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described.
- **Short-term right to reject:** 30 days for a full refund. For perishables such as flowers, it lasts only as long as the goods could reasonably be expected to last (s.22(4)).
- After that, the customer is entitled to repair or replacement, and then a price reduction or final rejection.
- A refund for faulty goods must be paid within 14 days of your agreeing to it.
- Services such as floral hire must be carried out with reasonable care and skill. Remedies are repeat performance or a price reduction.
- Terms that try to exclude these rights are not binding on consumers.

## 2. DMCC Act 2024 (consumer provisions in force 6 April 2025)

- **Enforcement:** the DMCC Act replaced the CPRs 2008. The CMA can now fine businesses directly, up to 10% of global turnover. First fines: AA Driving School, £4.2m (April 2026), and StubHub, about £889k. Both were for drip pricing. https://www.shma.co.uk/our-thoughts/cma-issues-pricing-fine-dmcca-businesses/ , https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/cma-issues-first-fines-under-its-new-uk-direct-consumer-enforcement-powers **(secondary)**
- **Fake reviews (Sch. 20 banned practices):**
  - Do not write, commission or selectively publish fake reviews.
  - Do not hide that a review was incentivised; disclose any free product or discount given for a review.
  - Do not suppress negative reviews.
  - Publish a short **review policy** explaining how you collect, check and remove reviews, and whether any were incentivised.
  - Take reasonable, proportionate steps to detect and remove fake reviews (CMA208: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf).
  - Make sure your Shopify review app's settings match the policy.
- **Drip pricing:** if a charge cannot be avoided, it must be in the headline price. If you have a **minimum unavoidable delivery fee** (for example, every order needs delivery), either include it in the price shown or show the full total upfront. Delivery charges that vary or are optional must be shown clearly, with how they are calculated, before checkout; a "Delivery charges" table linked from each product page works. https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-hidden-charges-clamping-down-on-drip-pricing , https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/full-price-no-surprises-cma-s-final-price-transparency-guidance-arrives/
- **Subscription contracts regime:** **not yet in force.** In August 2026 the government brought commencement forward to **January 2027** (previously expected spring 2027). The secondary legislation and guidance are still pending. https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/uk-government-accelerates-dmcca-subscription-reforms-to-january-2027/ , https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/15/key-takeaways-the-subscription-shake-up-are-you-ready-for-january-2027-102o14n **(secondary)**. For the flower subscription, prepare for:
  - "key information" shown upfront before purchase
  - pre-contract and post-contract notices
  - a 14-day cooling-off period at the start and again at each renewal of 12 months or more (refund minus a proportionate amount for goods supplied)
  - reminder notices before free or discounted periods end and before 6-monthly or annual renewals
  - **easy exit**, meaning cancellation online in a single step, with no "call us to cancel"

  Check the final rules once they are made **[Solicitor]**.

## 3. Business identity on the website

- **E-Commerce Regulations 2002, reg 6:** your name, a **geographic address**, email address, and VAT number if you are VAT-registered. All of this must be easy to find, for example in the footer and on the Contact page. A PO box is *not* a geographic address because it is not a place where the business is established. https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/the-uks-e-commerce-regulations , https://trustyourwebsite.com/uk/en/guides/ecommerce-regulations-2002-uk
- **Working from home:** if she does not want to publish her home address, she can use a virtual-office or service address where the business is genuinely reachable and post is handled, then confirm it with Trading Standards **[TS]**. She cannot use a PO box alone.
- **Sole trader trading as "ChoZen Boutique":** the Companies Act 2006 Part 41 business-names rules apply. Show her own name and a UK address for service on business documents, and display them on the website (for example "ChoZen Boutique is the trading name of [Full Name]").
- **Ltd company:** under the Company, LLP and Business (Names and Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015, show the full registered name, company number, "Registered in England and Wales" and the registered office address on the website and emails.

## 4. Data protection, cookies and marketing

- **Privacy notice (UK GDPR Arts 13/14)** must cover:
  - who you are and how to contact you
  - what data you collect (orders, recipient name and address, gift messages, payment details via Shopify Payments or Stripe, newsletter sign-ups)
  - why you use it and the lawful basis for each use: contract, legal obligation, legitimate interests, consent
  - who you share it with: Shopify, payment provider, couriers, email platform, analytics. Note that data goes outside the UK, mainly to Canada and the US, and the safeguards used
  - how long you keep it
  - people's rights: access, erasure, objection and so on
  - their right to complain to the ICO

  Recipients' data is collected through the buyer, so explain that too.
- **ICO data protection fee:** since 17 February 2025 the fees are Tier 1 (micro business) **£52**, Tier 2 **£78**, Tier 3 £3,763, with £5 off for direct debit. https://x.com/ICOnews/status/1891472805641806031 , https://www.wbw.co.uk/news/commercial/ico-data-protection-fee-increase-2025/
  - A business is exempt only if it processes data purely for its accounts and records, for advertising its own goods, or for staff administration.
  - Most online shops that use email marketing platforms, analytics or customer databases are expected to pay.
  - Use the ICO self-assessment (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/). In practice, budget **£52 a year**.
- **Cookies (PECR, as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025):**
  - From **5 February 2026**, first-party analytics used only for statistics no longer need consent, provided the site explains them clearly and offers an easy opt-out **(secondary)**: https://kukie.io/blog/uk-data-use-access-act-cookie-exemptions , https://www.cookiebot.com/en/uk-data-use-and-access-act-duaa/
  - Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, TikTok Pixel and any advertising or remarketing cookies **still need prior opt-in consent.**
  - Maximum PECR fines are now aligned with UK GDPR (£17.5m).
  - **Action:** turn on Shopify's cookie banner (Customer Privacy API, set to "UK/EEA: consent required"). Give "Accept" and "Reject" equal prominence and load no marketing pixels before consent. Publish a Cookie policy listing each cookie.
- **Marketing emails and SMS (PECR reg 22):**
  - **Soft opt-in** applies to existing customers if all of these hold: you got their details during a sale, you market only similar products, you gave an unticked opt-out box at checkout, and every message has an unsubscribe link. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/
  - Newsletter sign-ups from non-customers need clear, unticked opt-in consent.
  - **Never** market to the *gift recipient*: their details are for delivery only.

## 5. Crystal, astrology and jewellery claims (CAP Code and other law)

**Crystals.** CAP rules 12.1 and 12.6 cover medicinal and health claims. ASA guidance: "ASA/CAP have not seen evidence that crystal therapy can be used to treat medical conditions." https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-crystal-therapy.html
- **Rulings:**
  - *Spirit Walker Crystals* (17 April 2013): website implied crystals help with Alzheimer's, asthma, depression and heart conditions. Ruled misleading.
  - *Octopus Publishing Group* (19 October 2005): a general reference to the "healing power" of crystals was ruled misleading.
  - *GKOnlineCo t/a Aida Wellbeing* (24 April 2024): "relieved my meno-bloat" bracelet. Medical claims made the bracelet a medical device, so it needed MHRA registration. https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/news/asa-rulings-24-april-2024
- **Do not say:** heals, cures, treats, relieves, prevents, "reduces anxiety/stress", "for insomnia/depression/pain", "detoxifies", "boosts immunity", "EMF protection". Do not state as fact that a crystal "balances energy" or "aligns chakras" with a health effect.
- **Safer wording:** state belief or tradition, not effect. For example "Amethyst is traditionally associated with calm and is often chosen as a meditation companion" or "In crystal lore, rose quartz is known as the stone of love." Use "said to" and "believed by some to". Present any feeling of wellbeing as the customer's own experience. Do not link crystals to medical conditions at all.
- **Disclaimer** on crystal pages: "Crystals are sold as decorative and spiritual items. Their traditional meanings are not scientifically proven and are not a substitute for medical advice or treatment." A disclaimer will *not* rescue an explicit health claim (ASA approach).
- **Astrology and birthstones** (https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/psychics-spiritualists-fortune-tellers-astrologers-and-clairvoyants.html):
  - Birthstone and zodiac content is fine when framed as tradition.
  - Do not promise luck, wealth, love, protection or that a stone will change the buyer's life.
  - Do not exploit vulnerable people.
  - Do not use urgency or fear ("ward off bad luck").
  - "For entertainment" framing helps.

**Jewellery and the Hallmarking Act 1973.** It is an offence to describe an item as silver, gold, platinum or palladium unless it is hallmarked or under the exemption weight.
- Exemption weights: **silver 7.78g**, **gold 1g**, **palladium 1g**, **platinum 0.5g**. Above these weights the item must carry a UK Assay Office or convention hallmark. https://www.edinburghassayoffice.co.uk/complying-with-the-hallmarking-act/ , https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/miscellaneous/hallmarking
- Under the exemption weight, "925 sterling silver" may be used if the item is genuinely of that standard. Get supplier assay or test evidence.
- Do not call plated items "gold" or "silver". Use "gold-plated" or "silver-plated base metal".
- Online dealers should display the British Hallmarking Council **Dealer's Notice (online version B)** on the website (Assay Office guidance): https://www.assayoffice.co.uk/news/hallmarking-dealers-notice-goes-digital-alongside-launch-of-new-halo-award
- **Gemstones:**
  - Leaving out material information, such as treatments, can be a misleading omission under the DMCC Act Part 4 (the replacement for the CPRs).
  - Follow the CIBJO Blue Book and the NAJ Gemstone Guidance Note (2024, https://www.naj.co.uk/write/MediaUploads/Resources/NAJGuidanceNote-Gemstone2024.pdf):
    - disclose treatments (heated, dyed, irradiated, stabilised, reconstituted)
    - say "synthetic" or "lab-created" where applicable
    - do not use "genuine" or "natural" for imitation, dyed howlite sold as "turquoise", or reconstituted material
    - use "simulant" or "imitation" where that is what it is
  - Check what suppliers claim, since many crystal-bead bracelets are dyed **(secondary/[TS])**.

## 6. Balloons

- **Latex balloons are toys** under the Toys (Safety) Regulations 2011 and must meet EN 71-1. The packaging or listing should show:
  - "**Warning! Children under 8 years can choke or suffocate on uninflated or broken balloons. Adult supervision is required. Keep uninflated balloons away from children. Discard broken balloons at once.**"
  - "**Made of natural rubber latex**"

  Put both on the product page and on a card in the delivery. Buy from suppliers whose balloons carry UKCA/CE marking. https://law.resource.org/pub/eu/toys/en.71.1.2014.html
- **Foil balloons:** foil conducts electricity, and balloons caught on power lines cause outages and fires. Add the printed industry warnings: "Keep away from overhead power lines", "Always attach a weight", "Do not release outdoors". https://bapiaonline.com/news/blog-post-power-line-safety
- **Releases:** there is no national ban, but more than 100 councils ban releases on their land (https://www.mcsuk.org/what-we-do/pollution/marine-litter/plastic/dontletgo/dont-let-go-councils/). Never advertise or encourage releases.
- **Float-time claims** such as "floats for 7+ days" must be backed by evidence (CAP 3.7). Give typical ranges with conditions, for example "Latex: typically 12–24 hrs indoors; foil: typically 3–7 days."
- **Choking and helium:** advise against inhaling helium. Do not market balloons to children under 3 without adult-supervision messaging.

## 7. Flowers: lilies and pets (good practice, not a legal requirement)

All parts of true lilies (*Lilium*, *Hemerocallis*), including the pollen and the vase water, can cause fatal kidney failure in cats. https://www.petpoisonhelpline.com/poison/lilies/ , https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21147474/
- Add "Contains lilies: toxic to cats" on product pages.
- Add a delivery-option checkbox: "Recipient has cats: please substitute lilies".
- Put a care card in each delivery.
- Mention known plant allergens where relevant.

## 8. Page-by-page checklist

| Page | Mandatory or strongly advised items |
|---|---|
| **Footer (every page)** | Trading name; legal name (sole trader's own name or full Ltd name); geographic address (no PO box); email; phone; company number, "Registered in England and Wales" and registered office (if Ltd); VAT number (if registered); links to Terms, Privacy, Cookies, Returns |
| **Contact / About** | Everything in the footer; response times; honest story with no unverifiable "award-winning" or "best in Enfield" claims |
| **Delivery** | Same-day cut-off times; postcodes covered; delivery fees shown upfront (DMCC); UK shipping rates and timescales; substitution policy for flowers; what happens if the recipient is out; balloon delivery notes |
| **Returns & Refunds** | 14-day cancellation for non-perishables; exemptions named (fresh flowers, personalised items, unsealed hygiene items); who pays return postage; 14-day refund timeline; how to cancel; model cancellation form; separate statement of CRA rights for faulty goods (30 days, or less for perishables); how to report damaged flowers (photos within 24 hrs is acceptable as a *request*, not a condition that removes rights) |
| **Privacy** | Controller identity; data collected (including recipients); purposes and lawful bases; processors (Shopify, payment provider, couriers, email platform); international transfers; retention periods; individuals' rights; how to complain to the ICO |
| **Cookies** | List of each cookie with its purpose; analytics opt-out; consent banner with equal Accept/Reject for marketing cookies and no pixels before consent |
| **Terms** | Who the contract is with; ordering process; prices; substitutions; subscription terms (billing cycle, how to cancel online, renewals); floral hire terms (deposit, damage, cancellation, collection); liability (cannot exclude CRA rights); governing law of England and Wales |
| **Reviews policy** | How reviews are collected and checked; incentivised reviews labelled; negative reviews not suppressed |
| **Product pages** | Full price including VAT; key characteristics and sizes; "perishable: no cancellation" note on fresh flowers; lily and pet warning; latex choking and natural-rubber-latex warning; foil power-line warning; metal standard and hallmark status; gemstone treatment, synthetic or imitation disclosure; crystal "traditional meaning" wording and disclaimer; personalisation note that the item cannot be returned |
| **Checkout** | Total including delivery shown before payment; summary of the main characteristics; cancellation information; unticked marketing opt-out (soft opt-in) or opt-in; Terms link; "Pay now" / "Order with obligation to pay" button; confirmation email with all the information and the cancellation form |
| **Subscription page (by January 2027)** | Key information box (price, frequency, minimum term, renewal); one-step online cancellation; reminder emails before renewal; 14-day cooling-off (initial and renewal) |
| **Hallmarking** | Dealer's Notice B (online version) on the jewellery collection page or footer |

**Where to get professional sign-off:** subscription terms (from January 2027), the floral hire contract, the perishable exemption applied to subscriptions, a home address versus service address, and hallmarking or gem disclosure. Enfield Trading Standards and Business Companion (https://www.businesscompanion.info) are free starting points. A Primary Authority partnership gives assured advice for a fee.
