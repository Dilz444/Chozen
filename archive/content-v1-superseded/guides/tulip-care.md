## Why do tulips keep growing in the vase?

Tulip stems were still growing when they were cut, and they carry on. They can gain a centimetre or two a day,
which is why a neat bouquet can look wild by day three. They also bend towards the nearest light.

Neither means anything's wrong. Turn the vase a quarter turn each day to keep them even, and trim a little off the
stems when you change the water.

## How do I straighten droopy tulips?

Re-cut the stems, roll the bunch snugly in newspaper or kraft paper so the heads sit upright, and stand it in
deep, cool water for an hour or two. Take the paper off and they'll usually hold their shape. A tall vase that
supports most of the stem helps them stay that way.

If the heads have flopped and the stems feel soft, the tulips are thirsty or too warm. Cool water and a cooler room
fix most cases.

## How much water do tulips need?

Less than you'd think, but often. Tulips are thirsty and drink fast, so top up daily. Cool water from the tap is
better than lukewarm. Keep the leaves above the waterline, because tulip leaves go slimy quickly underwater.

## Can tulips go in a vase with daffodils?

Yes, with one step first. Freshly cut daffodils release a sap that shortens the life of other flowers. Stand the
daffodils in water on their own for at least six hours, ideally overnight, then add them to the tulips without
re-cutting them.

## Where should I keep tulips?

Somewhere cool, out of direct sun and well away from radiators and the fruit bowl. Tulips open wide in warmth, so
a warm room speeds them up. Move them somewhere cooler overnight and they'll last longer.

## Are tulips safe around cats and dogs?

Tulips can upset a pet's stomach, and the bulbs are the most toxic part. Cut tulips aren't as dangerous as lilies,
but keep them where a curious pet can't chew them. Our [flowers and pets guide](/blogs/guides/flowers-and-pets)
has the full list.
