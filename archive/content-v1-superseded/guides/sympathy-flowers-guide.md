## Should I send flowers to the home or to the funeral?

It depends on who they're for. Flowers for the funeral are a tribute to the person who has died, and they go to
the funeral director to travel with the coffin or be placed at the service. Flowers for the home are for the
people grieving, to say you're thinking of them.

You can send both. If you knew the person but not the family well, funeral flowers are usually right. If you're
close to the family, flowers at home often mean more.

## Should I check whether the family wants flowers?

Yes, before ordering funeral flowers. Look at the funeral notice or ask someone close. Some families ask for
"family flowers only", which means only close family send flowers to the service. Others ask for donations to a
charity instead. Following the family's wishes is the kindest thing you can do.

Flowers sent to the home are almost always welcome, whatever the funeral notice says.

## When should funeral flowers arrive?

Usually the day before the funeral, at the funeral director's. That gives them time to place the flowers with the
coffin or take them to the service. Check the funeral director's name, address and the date and time of the
service, and put the name of the person who has died on the order so the flowers reach the right family.

Some services ask for flowers to go straight to the church, chapel or crematorium. The funeral director can tell
you what's been arranged.

## When should I send flowers to the family's home?

Any time. In the first days after a death, the house is often busy with visitors, so flowers sent a week or
two later can feel especially welcome. There's no wrong moment.

## What kind of flowers should I send?

Something simple. White and cream are traditional, and soft colours such as pale pink, lilac and blue are just as
fitting. If you knew the person loved a particular flower or colour, that's a lovely choice.

For a home, a hand-tied bouquet that arrives in its own water is easiest, as it doesn't need arranging. Choose one
without lilies if there's a cat in the house, as lilies are dangerous to cats. Strongly scented flowers can be a
lot in a small room, so lighter scents are kinder.

For a funeral, a wreath, posy or simple arrangement from friends is usual. Letter tributes and casket sprays are
usually from close family.

## What should I write on a sympathy card?

A few words are enough. Say you're thinking of them, and use the name of the person who has died if you knew them.

- With deepest sympathy. Thinking of you all.
- Remembering David with love.
- So sorry for your loss. We're here whenever you need us.
- In loving memory of a dear friend.

For funeral flowers, the card is often addressed to the person who has died, such as "Mum, love you always" or
"Rest peacefully, Pat". There are more examples in our [card message ideas](/blogs/guides/what-to-write-in-a-card).
