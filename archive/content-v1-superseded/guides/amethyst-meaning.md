## What does it look like?

Amethyst ranges from pale lilac to a violet so deep it's nearly plum. It grows as six-sided points, often lining
the inside of a hollow rock called a geode, so a cluster looks like a bed of small purple teeth on a grey or
white base. Tumbled pieces are smooth and glassy. The colour is rarely even, and most stones show paler and
darker zones side by side.

## Where does it come from?

Amethyst is a variety of quartz (silicon dioxide), the same mineral as clear quartz. Its purple comes from small
amounts of iron in the crystal, changed over millions of years by low levels of natural radiation in the
surrounding rock. Most amethyst on sale comes from southern Brazil and northern Uruguay, where it formed inside
gas pockets in ancient lava flows. That's where the tall geodes come from. Zambia produces darker, redder
purples, and there's more in Madagascar and Mexico.

Heat changes amethyst to yellow or orange. Most of the citrine on the market is amethyst that's been heated,
and an honest label says "heat-treated".

## What is it traditionally associated with?

The name comes from the Greek *amethystos*, "not drunk". An old belief held that the stone kept its wearer sober,
and the story is a good one to write on a gift card. In crystal lore today, amethyst is associated with calm,
quiet and clear thinking. It has a long religious history too, and amethyst is the traditional stone in a
bishop's ring. In chakra tradition, its purple is matched with the crown chakra. Modern zodiac lists usually pair
it with Aquarius and Pisces.

## Who would you give it to?

It's the obvious gift for a February birthday, as a tumble stone, a cluster or a bead bracelet. A cluster suits
someone who's just moved and has an empty shelf to fill. Many people like to keep a small piece beside them while
they meditate or do yoga, and a point fits on a desk for someone starting a course. See the
[crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for what's in.

## How do you look after it?

Amethyst is 7 on the Mohs hardness scale, hard enough to scratch glass and fine for rings and bracelets worn
every day. The one thing it doesn't like is light. Strong direct sun fades the purple over months, so keep it out
of south-facing windows. Heat can change its colour too, so keep it away from radiators and hobs.

Dust clusters with a clean, soft make-up brush. Tumbled stones and beads can be wiped with a damp cloth or
rinsed in lukewarm soapy water, then dried. Bead bracelets on stretch cord last longer if you take them off
before a shower.
