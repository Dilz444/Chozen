## What does it look like?

Lapis lazuli is an intense royal to midnight blue, usually with tiny glinting flecks of gold-coloured pyrite and
sometimes white streaks of calcite. The most valued pieces are an even deep blue with a light scatter of gold.
Stones with more white look paler and denim-like. It's opaque, and polishes to a soft shine.

## Where does it come from?

Lapis lazuli is a rock rather than a single mineral. The blue is lazurite, mixed with calcite, pyrite and other
minerals, and it forms where limestone was changed by heat and pressure. Its Mohs hardness is about 5 to 5.5.
The most famous source is the Sar-e-Sang valley in Badakhshan, northern Afghanistan, where it has been mined
for more than six thousand years. Chile and the area around Lake Baikal in Russia also produce it.

Lapis is often treated. Waxing or oiling to improve the shine is common, and paler stones are sometimes dyed a
deeper blue. Sodalite and dyed jasper are sometimes sold as lapis. A careful seller states any treatment.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

Ancient Egyptians carved lapis into scarabs and jewellery, and it's set into Tutankhamun's burial mask. Ground
to a powder, it made ultramarine, the blue paint that Renaissance painters kept for the Virgin Mary's robes
because it could cost more than gold. In crystal lore, lapis is linked with wisdom, truth and honest speech. In chakra
tradition it's matched with the throat and third-eye chakras, and modern zodiac lists often pair it with Libra
and Sagittarius.

## Who would you give it to?

A September birthday, as an alternative to sapphire. A graduate, a teacher or anyone finishing a long course,
with a card about wisdom. Someone who loves art history or a museum trip. A lapis pendant or earrings suit people
who wear gold, which picks up the pyrite flecks. See the [crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for
current pieces.

## How do you look after it?

Lapis is softer than quartz and slightly porous, so it scratches and absorbs more than most stones here. Wipe it
with a soft, damp cloth and dry it straight away. Don't soak it, and keep it away from perfume, hairspray,
household cleaners and anything acidic such as lemon juice, which can etch the calcite.

Put lapis jewellery on last, after creams and scent, and take it off for washing up and swimming. Store it on its
own in a soft pouch so harder stones don't scratch it.
