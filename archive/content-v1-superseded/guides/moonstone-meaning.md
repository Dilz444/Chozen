## What does it look like?

Moonstone is milky and semi-transparent, with a soft light that drifts across the stone as it moves. The body
colour can be colourless, white, grey, peach or a pale champagne. The finest pieces are nearly clear with an
electric blue glow. It's usually cut as a smooth dome, called a cabochon, because a rounded top shows the glow best.

## Where does it come from?

Moonstone is a feldspar, made of two feldspar minerals (orthoclase and albite) that separated into very thin
alternating layers as the rock cooled. Light scattering between those layers makes the glow, which is called
adularescence after adularia, a feldspar named for the Adula mountains in the Swiss Alps. Sri Lanka has been the
classic source for centuries. Moonstone is also mined in southern India, Madagascar, Myanmar and Tanzania.

The stone sold as rainbow moonstone is white labradorite, a related feldspar. Both are natural. They're just
different minerals, and the label should say which.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

The Romans associated it with the moon, and an old belief held that it was made of solidified moonlight. In
India it has long been a sacred stone and a traditional wedding gift. In crystal lore it's associated with new
beginnings, intuition and the cycles of the moon, and in folklore it was a travellers' stone. Art Nouveau
jewellers, René Lalique among them, set it in pieces at the turn of the twentieth century. Modern zodiac lists
usually pair it with Cancer.

## Who would you give it to?

Someone with a June birthday who'd prefer a stone to a pearl. A bride, as a quiet something new. A friend going
travelling or moving away, with a note about the travellers' stone. Moonstone earrings or a pendant suit someone
who wears silver and soft colours. See the [crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for loose stones and the
[birthstones guide](/blogs/guides/birthstones-by-month) for the other months.

## How do you look after it?

Moonstone is 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale and splits along flat planes inside the stone, so a hard knock can crack
it. Take rings off for gardening, cleaning and the gym. Store it in a soft pouch away from harder stones and
metal, which scratch the polish.

Clean it in lukewarm soapy water with a soft brush, rinse and dry. Don't use an ultrasonic or steam jewellery
cleaner, and avoid sudden changes of temperature. Daylight doesn't fade it.
