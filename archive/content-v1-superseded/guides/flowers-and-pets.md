## Why are lilies so dangerous to cats?

A tiny amount can do serious harm. A cat can be poisoned by nibbling a leaf, drinking the vase water or licking
pollen off its fur after brushing past the flowers. True lilies (the *Lilium* family, such as Easter, Oriental,
Asiatic and tiger lilies) and day lilies are the ones to keep out of a home with a cat. Cutting off the pollen
doesn't make them safe.

If you think a cat has touched a lily, call a vet straight away. Don't wait for symptoms, because the sooner a vet
sees the cat, the better its chances.

Lilies are much less of a worry for dogs, though they can still cause an upset stomach.

## Which other flowers should I avoid?

These common bouquet flowers can make cats or dogs ill if eaten:

- **Lily of the valley.** Seriously toxic to both cats and dogs, as it affects the heart.
- **Daffodils.** Cause vomiting and stomach pain. The bulbs are worst, but stems and vase water count too.
- **Tulips.** Can upset the stomach, and the bulbs are the most toxic part.
- **Chrysanthemums.** Usually cause vomiting, drooling and diarrhoea.
- **Hydrangeas.** Can cause vomiting if chewed.
- **Amaryllis and foxgloves.** Both toxic, and foxgloves affect the heart.
- **Calla lilies and peace lilies.** Not true lilies, but they irritate the mouth and throat.
- **Eucalyptus.** A favourite in bouquets, and it can upset a pet's stomach.

## Which flowers are safer for homes with pets?

Roses, sunflowers, gerberas, snapdragons, freesias, orchids and asters are generally considered non-toxic to cats
and dogs. Even so, a pet that eats a lot of any plant can be sick, so keep vases out of reach and change the water
often.

## What should I ask for when ordering flowers?

Say there's a cat or dog in the house and ask for no lilies, no lily of the valley and no daffodils. Any florist
can swap them for something else. If you're sending flowers as a surprise and don't know, it's safest to leave
lilies out anyway.
