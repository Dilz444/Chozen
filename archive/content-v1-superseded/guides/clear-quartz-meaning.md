## What does it look like?

Clear quartz is colourless and glassy. A natural point has six sides and a six-faced tip, and many have horizontal
lines across the sides. Few are perfectly clear. Most have white wisps, internal cracks that throw small
rainbows, or tiny inclusions of other minerals. Clusters are several points growing from a shared base.

## Where does it come from?

Quartz is silicon dioxide and one of the most common minerals in the Earth's crust. Large clear crystals grow
slowly in open cavities and veins where hot, silica-rich water cools over a long time. Most clear quartz on sale
comes from Brazil and Madagascar. Arkansas in the US is famous for it, and the Alps and the Himalayas have
produced fine crystals for centuries.

Quartz also keeps time. When squeezed it produces a small electric charge, and that is what a quartz watch uses
to tick at a steady rate. The quartz in watches today is grown in factories rather than mined.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

The word "crystal" comes from the Greek *krystallos*, meaning ice, because the ancient Greeks thought rock crystal
was water frozen so hard it could never melt. In crystal lore, clear quartz is the "master crystal", associated
with clarity and with amplifying the meaning of stones placed beside it. In chakra tradition it's matched with
the crown chakra. Many people like to keep a point on their desk as a reminder of something they're working
towards. Rock crystal balls were used for scrying in medieval Europe, and a few survive in museum collections.

## Who would you give it to?

An April birthday, as a lower-cost alternative to diamond. A graduate or someone starting a new role, as a desk
point with a card about new beginnings. Someone who already collects crystals, because a clear point sits well
next to any colour. A sphere or cluster also suits a bright room as a light-catcher. See the
[crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for current pieces.

## How do you look after it?

Clear quartz is 7 on the Mohs scale, so it's tough enough for rings and pendants. Points can chip at the tip if
knocked, so stand them where they won't be brushed off a shelf. Wash in lukewarm soapy water and dry with a soft
cloth. A dusty cluster cleans up with a soft brush.

Unlike amethyst and rose quartz, clear quartz won't fade. The risk with a sphere is fire. A polished ball in
direct sun focuses light like a lens and can scorch wood or fabric, so keep spheres out of sunny windows.
