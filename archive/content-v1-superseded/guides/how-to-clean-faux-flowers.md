## What are my faux flowers made of?

Check before you clean, because the material decides what's safe. Most faux flowers are one of three things.

- **Fabric, often called silk.** Usually polyester. Soft petals with a slight sheen. Water can make the dye run or leave a ring.
- **Real touch.** A foam or latex-feel petal that looks damp and waxy, common on roses, tulips and peonies. Takes a gently damp cloth.
- **Plastic.** Firm, often greenery, succulents and berries. The easiest to clean.

Most arrangements mix them. Clean to the most delicate material in the vase.

## What's the safest everyday way to dust them?

A soft, dry brush. A clean make-up brush, a small paintbrush or a feather duster gets into the folds without
bending anything. Work from the top flower down, holding each head steady with your other hand. Do it every week
or two and the dust never gets a chance to settle in.

A microfibre cloth does the same job on leaves and stems.

## Can I use a hairdryer?

Yes, on the cool setting only. Hold it about 30cm away and start on the lowest speed, blowing from the back of the
arrangement forward. It shifts dust from the middle of a dense bunch better than a brush can. Hot air can soften
glue and warp plastic, so never switch it up.

Do this outside or over a sink if the flowers are dusty, or the dust lands on the shelf instead.

## Does the salt-bag method work?

It works for sturdy fabric flowers with ingrained dust, but it has risks. Put the flower heads in a large bag with
a few tablespoons of coarse salt, hold the bag closed around the stems and shake gently for a minute. The salt
scrubs the petals and takes the dust with it.

The risks are real, though:

- Salt can scuff fine fabric and take the shine off it.
- It can knock off glitter, flocking or painted detail.
- Grains lodge in the centres of roses and peonies and are hard to shift.
- On real-touch petals it can leave scratches.

Try it on one stem first, use a cool hairdryer afterwards to clear the grains, and skip it altogether for
anything precious.

## Can faux flowers get wet?

Plastic ones can. Rinse plastic greenery under a cool tap and let it dry on a towel. Real-touch petals take a cloth
wrung out in plain water, wiped gently, then left to air dry.

Fabric flowers are a different matter. Water can cause marks and loosen the glue that holds the petals to the
stem, and wet wire stems can rust inside their wrapping. If a fabric petal has a mark, dab it with a barely damp
cotton bud and dry it straight away with a cool hairdryer.

## What should I avoid?

- Soaking anything, including in soapy water.
- Bleach, furniture polish, glass cleaner or household sprays.
- Dishwashers and washing machines.
- Hot air, radiators and steam.
- Direct sun, which fades colour faster than anything else.
- Pulling petals to straighten them. Bend the wire under the head instead.

## How should I store faux flowers?

Store them heads up in a box big enough that nothing touches the lid, with acid-free tissue between stems. Keep
the box somewhere dry and at room temperature. Lofts and garages get hot in summer and damp in winter, which is
hard on glue and wire.

Before you put them away, dust them, so dirt doesn't set in while they're stored. When you take them out, give
the stems a gentle bend back into shape and a cool blast with the hairdryer to fluff the petals.
