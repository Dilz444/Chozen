## What do teachers actually want at the end of term?

A card that says something specific. Ask teachers what they keep, and most mention the notes from children and
parents more than any gift. A sentence about what your child loved this year, or something the teacher did that
made a difference, means a great deal.

If you're adding a gift, small and useful beats big and fragile.

## Are fresh flowers a good teacher gift?

They're a lovely gesture, with one catch. On the last day of term a class teacher may get several bouquets, and
then has to carry them home, sometimes on the bus, before a summer holiday when they might be away. Big bouquets
in water bags can be hard to manage.

If you'd like to give fresh flowers, go for a compact, hand-tied posy, or arrange delivery a day or two before
the last day so they can enjoy them in the classroom. Long-lasting flowers such as alstroemeria, carnations and
chrysanthemums get through the holidays better than roses.

## Should parents club together for a class gift?

It's common, and it saves the teacher a pile of small gifts. One parent collects, everyone gives what they can
without pressure, and the class signs one big card. Keep it modest. Many schools have a gifts policy for staff,
and a very expensive present can put a teacher in an awkward spot.

## What should I write in a teacher's card?

Be specific. Mention a moment, a subject or a change you've seen in your child.

- Thank you for making Maths Lily's favourite lesson. We never thought we'd see the day.
- Thank you for your patience this year. Leo has grown so much in confidence.
- Thank you for every reading record signed and every worry listened to.
- You've made this year a happy one. Enjoy a well-earned summer.
- Thank you from all of us in Year 3. We'll miss you.
- Thank you for seeing the best in Amir, even on the hard days.
