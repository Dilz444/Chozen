## What does it look like?

Labradorite is a dark grey to grey-green stone that looks plain until it catches the light. Then a sheet of
colour flashes across it, usually electric blue, often with green, gold or copper, and rarely purple or pink.
Polished palm stones, spheres and slabs show it best because the flat or curved face lines up with the layers
inside. A piece with flash across most of its face costs more than one with a small patch.

## Where does it come from?

Labradorite is a plagioclase feldspar, a calcium and sodium aluminium silicate. As it cooled very slowly in deep
igneous rock, it separated into microscopic layers of slightly different make-up. Light reflecting between those
layers produces the colour, which gemmologists call labradorescence.

It's named after Labrador in Canada, where European missionaries described it from Paul's Island in the
late eighteenth century. Most labradorite on sale today comes from Madagascar. Finland produces a dark, very
colourful form sold as spectrolite, and there are deposits in Norway, Russia and the US.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

A story often told in Labrador says the northern lights were once trapped in the rocks of the coast until a
warrior struck them with his spear and set most of them free. In crystal lore, labradorite is linked with change,
transformation and intuition, and it's traditionally given at a turning point. In chakra tradition it's matched
with the throat and third-eye chakras, and modern zodiac lists usually pair it with Scorpio.

## How do you look after it?

Labradorite is 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale, softer than quartz, and it splits cleanly along flat planes if it's
knocked hard. Rings are best kept for occasions, and pendants and earrings take less wear. Store it apart from
harder stones, which can scratch the polish.

Wipe it with a soft, damp cloth and dry it. Avoid household cleaners, ultrasonic jewellery cleaners and sudden
changes of temperature. Sunlight won't fade the flash, so a polished piece can sit wherever the light shows it best.
