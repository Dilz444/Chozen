## What is a hallmark?

A set of marks stamped on precious metal by an independent Assay Office after testing it. In the UK, an item can only be described as gold, silver, platinum or palladium if it's hallmarked, unless it weighs less than the exemption weight: 1g for gold and palladium, 0.5g for platinum and 7.78g for silver. A UK hallmark always includes:

- the **sponsor's mark**, showing who sent the piece for testing
- the **fineness mark**, showing the metal and its purity, such as 925 for sterling silver
- the **Assay Office mark**, showing which office tested it

## What does "925" mean on a listing?

The piece is sterling silver, which is 92.5% pure silver. Pieces over 7.78g carry a UK hallmark. Lighter pieces may not be hallmarked, and the listing says so.

## What does "plated" mean on a listing?

A thin layer of gold or silver over a base metal such as brass. We always write "gold-plated" or "silver-plated", never "gold" or "silver" alone, because the piece isn't solid precious metal. Plating can wear over time with use.

## Are the gemstones treated?

It depends on the stone. Each listing tells you if it has been dyed, heated, stabilised or reconstituted, or if it's lab-made or an imitation [to confirm per item].
