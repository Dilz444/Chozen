## What does it look like?

Most selenite on sale is milky white with a silky, striped sheen that runs along the length of the piece. Light
travels along the fibres, so a tower or lamp glows when a light is behind it. It's sold as towers, wands, flat
plates, bowls and rough sticks. True selenite crystals look quite different, clear and glassy in flat blades, and
they're less often seen in gift shops.

## Where does it come from?

Selenite is gypsum, a hydrated calcium sulphate, the same mineral used to make plaster. It forms where salty
water evaporates, in old lake and sea beds. Gypsum is common worldwide, but most of the white satin pieces in
shops come from Morocco. Mexico is famous for it too. The Cave of the Crystals at Naica holds gypsum crystals
more than ten metres long. There are good deposits in the US and in Britain as well, in Nottinghamshire,
Cumbria and Sussex.

Strictly, the silky, fibrous white material is called satin spar and the clear, bladed crystals are selenite.
In crystal shops both are usually sold as selenite.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

The name comes from Selene, the Greek moon goddess, after its soft, pale sheen. In crystal lore, selenite is
associated with clarity, calm and light, and it's said to cleanse other crystals. Many people like to rest their
tumble stones and jewellery on a selenite plate overnight for that reason. In chakra tradition it's matched with
the crown chakra.

## Who would you give it to?

Someone who already keeps crystals, as a plate or bowl to put them on. A friend moving into a new flat, as a
tower or lamp for a bookshelf. Someone who likes clean, white interiors more than bright colours. A selenite
plate also makes a pretty dish for rings by the bed. See the [crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for
current pieces.

## How do you look after it?

Selenite needs more care than any other stone on this list. It's about 2 on the Mohs scale, soft enough to
scratch with a fingernail, and the fibres can splinter at the ends. Hold it gently and stand it where it won't
fall.

Keep it dry. Gypsum dissolves slowly in water, so never wash it, soak it, cleanse it in salt water or keep it
in a bathroom. Dust it with a dry, soft cloth. Damp air will dull the surface over time, so a living room or
bedroom suits it better than a kitchen. Keep it out of reach of small children and pets.
