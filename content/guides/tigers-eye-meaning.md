## What does it look like?

Tiger's eye is striped in honey gold, caramel and dark brown. Polished, it shows a bright band of light that
slides across the surface as you tilt it, like light on a cat's eye. The effect is strongest on domed and rounded
stones, so it's usually sold tumbled, as beads, or cut into smooth cabochons. Rough pieces look dull and fibrous
until they're polished.

## Where does it come from?

Tiger's eye is quartz grown through a fibrous blue mineral called crocidolite, a form of riebeckite. Iron in
those fibres later turned to golden-brown iron oxides, which give the colour. The fibres are locked inside the
quartz, so polished stones are hard (about 7 on the Mohs scale) and safe to handle. Most tiger's eye comes from
the Northern Cape in South Africa. There's more in Western Australia, Namibia, India and China.

Where the iron didn't change, the stone stays blue-grey and is sold as hawk's eye. Heating golden tiger's eye
turns it red to mahogany, so most red tiger's eye has been heat-treated.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

Tiger's eye is traditionally associated with courage, confidence and a steady nerve. A story often told about
it says Roman soldiers carried it into battle. In folklore it was carried for good fortune too. In chakra
tradition its gold is matched with the solar plexus chakra, and modern zodiac lists usually pair it with Leo.

## Who would you give it to?

Someone sitting exams, a driving test or an interview, as a good-luck token for a pocket. Someone starting a new
job or a new school. It's also one of the crystals men are most often given, as a bead bracelet or a single
polished stone for a desk, and it's a common pick for Father's Day. See the
[crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for current pieces.

## How do you look after it?

Tiger's eye is tough enough for bracelets worn every day. Wipe it with a damp cloth or rinse it in lukewarm soapy
water, then dry it. Bead bracelets on stretch cord last longer if you take them off before a shower or a swim, as
water weakens the elastic.

Daylight doesn't fade it. Avoid sanding, drilling or cutting rough pieces at home, because the dust from the
unsealed fibres shouldn't be breathed in. Finished, polished pieces are fine.
