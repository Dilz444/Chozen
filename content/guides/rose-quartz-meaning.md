## What does it look like?

Rose quartz runs from the faintest blush to a soft bubblegum pink. It's translucent rather than clear, with a
cloudy, milky glow. It almost never grows as visible pointed crystals. It forms in solid masses instead, which is
why it's usually sold tumbled, carved into hearts and spheres, or as rough chunks. Some polished spheres show a
faint six-rayed star when a single bright light shines on them.

## Where does it come from?

Rose quartz is quartz (silicon dioxide), hardness 7, the same mineral as amethyst and clear quartz. The pink was
long blamed on titanium or manganese. Studies of the stone under electron microscopes found microscopic fibres of
a pink mineral close to dumortierite threaded through the quartz, and those fibres carry the colour. It forms in
pegmatites, which are coarse-grained veins in granite. Most comes from Brazil and Madagascar, and it's also mined
in South Africa, Mozambique and the Black Hills of South Dakota, where it's the state mineral.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

In crystal lore, rose quartz is the stone of love: romantic love, but also friendship, family and kindness to
yourself. Some modern retellings of Greek myth link it with Aphrodite. In chakra tradition, pink and green stones
are matched with the heart chakra. That's why rose quartz is so often carved into hearts, and why it turns up in
Valentine's, Mother's Day and anniversary gifts.

## Who would you give it to?

A partner, on Valentine's Day or an anniversary. Your mum, for Mother's Day or a birthday. A best friend who's
had a hard year, as a small gesture that says you're thinking of them. A heart or a tumble stone fits inside a
card envelope. A larger sphere or a raw chunk suits a dressing table. A new home is another good moment. See the
[crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for what's in, or the [crystals by colour](/blogs/guides/crystals-by-colour)
guide for other pinks.

## How do you look after it?

Rose quartz is hard enough for everyday jewellery and won't scratch easily. Keep it out of strong direct sun,
because its colour can pale slowly on a bright windowsill. Rinse it in lukewarm water with a drop of washing-up
liquid and dry it with a soft cloth. Facial rollers need the same wash after each use, and a soft pouch stops the
stone chipping against other things in a drawer.

Hearts and spheres are tough but can chip if dropped on tiles. Keep small pieces away from young children and pets.
