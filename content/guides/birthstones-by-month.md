## What is the January birthstone?

Garnet. Most garnets sold are deep wine red, though the garnet family also comes in green, orange and pink.
Victorian jewellers set Bohemian garnets in clusters for brooches and rings, and garnet is traditionally a
stone of friendship and loyalty, which makes it a fitting gift for a close friend's January birthday.

## What is the February birthstone?

Amethyst, purple quartz with a Greek name meaning "not drunk". It's traditionally associated with calm, and a
small cluster or tumble stone suits almost anyone. The [amethyst guide](/blogs/guides/amethyst-meaning) has care
notes, including why it shouldn't sit on a sunny windowsill.

## What is the March birthstone?

Aquamarine, the sea-blue variety of beryl, whose name is Latin for "seawater". In folklore it was a sailors'
stone. The UK alternative is bloodstone, a dark green chalcedony spotted with red iron oxide, once carved into
seals and signet rings. Aquamarine suits someone who loves the coast.

## What is the April birthstone?

Diamond, pure carbon and the hardest natural mineral. The UK alternative is rock crystal, which is clear quartz,
and it gives the same colourless sparkle for a fraction of the price. The
[clear quartz guide](/blogs/guides/clear-quartz-meaning) explains where it comes from.

## What is the May birthstone?

Emerald, a green beryl coloured by chromium or vanadium, with Colombia and Zambia the best-known sources. Almost
all emeralds are oiled to hide small cracks, which a good jeweller discloses. The UK alternative is chrysoprase,
an apple-green chalcedony coloured by nickel and mostly mined in Australia. Both suit a spring birthday.

## What is the June birthstone?

Pearl, the only birthstone made by a living creature. Most pearls today are cultured, grown in farmed molluscs.
The UK alternative is moonstone, a feldspar with a soft floating glow (see the
[moonstone guide](/blogs/guides/moonstone-meaning)). The US list also gives alexandrite, which changes from green
in daylight to purplish red under lamplight.

## What is the July birthstone?

Ruby, red corundum coloured by chromium and second only to diamond in hardness. Most rubies are heated to improve
their colour. The UK alternative is carnelian, a translucent orange chalcedony used for Roman seal rings (see the
[carnelian guide](/blogs/guides/carnelian-meaning)).

## What is the August birthstone?

Peridot, the lime-green gem form of olivine, which forms deep in the Earth and reaches the surface in volcanic
rock. The UK alternative is sardonyx, a banded chalcedony in red-brown and white layers, the classic stone for
carved cameos. The US list adds spinel. Peridot's bright green suits a summer birthday.

## What is the September birthstone?

Sapphire, blue corundum coloured by iron and titanium. Sapphires come in most colours apart from red, which is
called ruby. Many are heated to improve colour. The UK alternative is lapis lazuli, the deep blue rock flecked with
gold-coloured pyrite (see the [lapis lazuli guide](/blogs/guides/lapis-lazuli-meaning)).

## What is the October birthstone?

Opal, a hardened silica gel that holds water inside it. Precious opal flashes with colour because light bends
between tiny spheres of silica. Most comes from Australia, and Ethiopia is now a major source. Opal is soft and
can crack if it dries out or meets sudden heat, so it suits earrings and pendants better than everyday rings. The
US list also gives pink tourmaline.

## What is the November birthstone?

Topaz. Natural topaz is often golden, sherry or pale blue, and the bright sky and London blue topaz sold widely
is colourless topaz that has been irradiated and heated. Citrine, yellow quartz, is listed beside it on both the
UK and US lists (see the [citrine guide](/blogs/guides/citrine-meaning)). Warm autumn colours for an autumn
birthday.

## What is the December birthstone?

Turquoise, a sky-blue to green copper mineral mined in Iran for thousands of years and in the American
Southwest. Much turquoise is stabilised with resin, and dyed howlite is often sold in its place, so the label
matters. Tanzanite, a blue-violet zoisite found only near Arusha in northern Tanzania, is listed beside it. Older
UK charts give lapis lazuli as December's alternative.

## How do you give a birthstone as a present?

Pick the stone first, then the form. A tumble stone or small crystal in a card is the simplest. A bead bracelet
or pendant is something they'll wear. Write the month and its traditional meaning on the card. Shop by stone in the
[crystals collection](/collections/crystals) and [gemstone jewellery](/collections/gemstone-jewellery), where each
jewellery description says what the metal is and whether the stone is treated.
