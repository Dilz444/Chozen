## What does it look like?

Citrine runs from pale lemon to honey, amber and a deep burnt orange. Natural citrine is usually soft yellow,
sometimes with a smoky tint, and even in colour through the stone. Most citrine clusters on sale look different,
with bright orange tips fading to milky white where the points meet the base. That pattern is a sign of heating.

## Where does it come from?

Citrine is quartz (silicon dioxide), hardness 7, coloured by traces of iron. Natural citrine is uncommon. It's
mined in Brazil, Madagascar, Zambia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Russia's Ural Mountains have
produced it too.

Most citrine on the market, and nearly all the deep orange clusters, starts life as amethyst. Heated to a few
hundred degrees, amethyst loses its purple and turns yellow, orange or reddish brown. Smoky quartz can be heated
to yellow as well. The result is real quartz with a lasting colour, but it isn't natural citrine, and an honest
label says "heat-treated". Ask any seller which one you're buying.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

The name comes from the French *citron*, lemon. In folklore, citrine is the "merchant's stone", and some
shopkeepers still keep a piece by the till as a token of good fortune. In crystal lore it's associated with
warmth, cheerfulness and confidence, and it's said to be one stone that never needs cleansing. In chakra
tradition its yellow is matched with the solar plexus chakra. Modern zodiac lists often pair it with Gemini
and Leo.

Someone opening a shop, starting a business or starting a new job, with a card about the merchant's stone. A
November birthday, for anyone using the US list or who'd like a lower-cost stone than topaz. A friend who loves
yellow, or someone with a sunny kitchen windowsill. A point or small cluster suits a desk. See the

## How do you look after it?

Citrine is hard enough for rings and everyday bracelets. Natural citrine can pale in years of strong sun, so keep
it out of bright windows. Heat-treated citrine is steadier in daylight but still best on a shelf rather than a
sill. Keep all citrine away from high heat, which can change its colour again.

Wash it in lukewarm soapy water and dry it with a soft cloth. Clusters clean up with a soft brush. Points can chip
if knocked, so give them a spot at the back of the shelf.
