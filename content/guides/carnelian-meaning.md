## What does it look like?

Carnelian ranges from pale apricot to deep orange, red and reddish brown. Hold a good piece up to a window and
light glows through it like marmalade. Polished, it has a waxy shine. The colour is usually a little uneven and
cloudy, with soft patches rather than sharp stripes.

## Where does it come from?

Carnelian is chalcedony, a form of quartz made of crystals far too small to see, with a Mohs hardness of about
6.5 to 7. Its colour comes from iron oxide. India has been the great source for thousands of years, and the
bead-makers of Khambhat in Gujarat still work it. Brazil, Uruguay and Madagascar supply much of today's
market.

A lot of carnelian sold is agate that's been dyed or heated. Heating to deepen the colour is an old practice and
the colour lasts. Dyed agate is a different thing, often grey or white stone soaked in colour, and it should be
labelled dyed. If a piece is bright orange in hard, regular stripes, ask the seller.

## What is it traditionally associated with?

Carnelian is one of the oldest worked stones. Ancient Egyptians set it in jewellery and amulets, and Roman seal
rings were often carved in it because hot wax doesn't stick to the surface. In crystal lore it's a stone of
courage, warmth and creativity. In chakra tradition its orange is matched with the sacral chakra, and modern
zodiac lists often pair it with Aries and Leo.

## Who would you give it to?

A July birthday, as a gentler-priced alternative to ruby. A friend who paints, writes or makes things, as a desk
stone for a new project. Someone starting a business or training for a big event, with a note about courage.

## How do you look after it?

Carnelian is hard enough for everyday rings and bracelets. Wash it in lukewarm soapy water and dry it with a soft
cloth. It's slightly porous, so keep it away from perfume, hairspray and household cleaners, which can dull the
polish over time.

Natural and heated carnelian hold their colour in daylight. Dyed stones can fade over a long time in strong sun,
so they're best kept out of sunny windows. Store it away from harder stones such as sapphire or diamond.
