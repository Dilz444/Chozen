## What does it look like?

Black tourmaline is jet black with a glassy shine on its crystal faces. Raw crystals are long and slender, with
fine parallel ridges running from end to end, and a cross-section that looks like a rounded triangle. Rough
pieces are often still set in pale quartz or with flakes of silvery mica. Tumbled stones lose the ridges and
become smooth, dense black pebbles.

## Where does it come from?

Tourmaline is a family of boron silicate minerals, and schorl is the black, iron-rich member of it. It's the most
common tourmaline by far. It crystallises in granites and in pegmatites, the coarse veins that form as granite
cools. In Britain it's common in the granites of Cornwall and Devon. Most pieces sold come from Brazil,
Madagascar, Namibia and Pakistan.

Tourmaline has a curious habit. Warm it and one end gains a small electric charge. Eighteenth-century Dutch
traders noticed it pulling ash out of their pipes and called it *aschentrekker*, "ash-puller".

## What is it traditionally associated with?

In crystal lore, black tourmaline is traditionally regarded as a protective and grounding stone. That's why many
people like to keep a raw piece by the front door or on a hall table. In chakra tradition, black and dark stones
are matched with the root chakra. Modern zodiac lists often place it with Capricorn. Pink tourmaline is on the US
list as an October birthstone, but black tourmaline isn't a birthstone in the UK or the US.

## Who would you give it to?

Someone moving into a new home, with a note about the front-door tradition. A friend who likes dark,
architectural objects more than pastel ones, because a raw crystal looks like a small sculpture. A colleague
starting a demanding new role, as a desk stone. Black tourmaline also sits well in a mixed gift beside rose quartz
or clear quartz. See the [crystals collection](/collections/crystals) for current pieces.

## How do you look after it?

Black tourmaline is 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale, so it's hard, but raw crystals are brittle along their length
and can snap if dropped. Keep them on a flat surface and pick them up by the thick end. Raw pieces with mica can
shed glittery flakes, which is normal.

Dust with a soft brush. Tumbled stones can be rinsed in lukewarm water and dried straight away. Keep it away from
strong heat, and don't put raw pieces in water for long, because any mica or matrix around the crystal can loosen.
