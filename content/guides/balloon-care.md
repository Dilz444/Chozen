## Why do foil balloons last longer than latex?

Latex is porous, so helium slowly escapes through the rubber. Foil is made of a metallised film that holds helium
in far better. That's why a foil balloon can still be floating days after the latex ones have dropped.

Bigger latex balloons float longer than small ones, and some latex balloons have a gel coating inside that slows
the helium escaping. Once a balloon starts to sink, it won't recover.

## What does heat do to balloons?

Heat makes helium expand. A balloon in direct sun, near a radiator or in a hot car can stretch until it pops, and
latex gets thinner and leaks faster. Keep balloons in a shaded room and never leave them in a parked car on a warm
day.

## What does cold do to balloons?

Cold makes helium shrink, so balloons look soft and sag, especially foil ones. Bring them into a warm room and they
usually fill out again within an hour. They haven't lost helium, it has only contracted.

## Can I take balloons outside?

For a short time, and always weighted. Wind and sun shorten their life, and rain can leave marks. Keep them tied to
a weight at all times.

Keep foil balloons well away from overhead power lines. Foil conducts electricity, and balloons caught in power
lines cause power cuts and fires.

## Is it all right to release balloons?

No. Never release balloons outdoors. They come down as litter on land and in the sea, where animals can eat them
or get tangled in the string, and many councils ban releases on their land. When you've finished with a balloon,
cut it near the knot or valve to let the helium out, and put it in the bin.

## Are latex balloons safe for children?

With an adult watching, yes. Latex balloons carry this warning.

**Warning! Children under 8 years can choke or suffocate on uninflated or broken balloons. Adult supervision is
required. Keep uninflated balloons away from children. Discard broken balloons at once.**

Ribbon can also be a hazard for babies and toddlers, so keep strings short and out of reach of cots.

## What if someone has a latex allergy?

Choose foil instead. Latex balloons are labelled "Made of natural rubber latex", and natural rubber latex can
cause reactions in people with a latex allergy, from a rash to a serious reaction. Even a balloon in the same room can be a problem for someone who
is very sensitive, so ask before sending latex to a hospital, nursery or a person you don't know well.
